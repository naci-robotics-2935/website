Robots Over The Years

Every January we get a new game and about six weeks to design, build and program a robot for it.

2025  /  Reefscape

Azure

Game
Analysis

A new season, a new robot. It all started with an in-depth game analysis in January. We decided on a simple, reliable design: a hybrid robot with a two stage elevator and an active intake attached to the arm, built for fast scoring and consistent performance throughout matches.

Drivebase and Power

With an open-field game demanding precision and agility, we knew swerve was the drivetrain to go with. MAXSwerve modules powered by NEO motors for drive and Baby NEOs for steering. 2025 was our first year competing with a full swerve system after testing the modules in the off-season.

Elevator

Single stage belt rigged elevator with a maximum extension of 30 inches. Uses an open loop belt in combination with custom 3D printed belt tensioners, run by independent NEO Vortex motors on a 1:9 planetary ratio.

Intake

Internal roller intake design powered by a NEO motor, using 2 inch wheels on a 9:1 ratio, attached to the arm for fast pickup.

2024  /  Crescendo

Crescendo

Game
Analysis

A new season brought a new robot. After analyzing Crescendo, we committed to a simple, reliable, low-profile design built for consistent Speaker scoring. We built a hybrid robot with a ground intake and a compact shooter, focused on fast cycles and a strong, dependable auto for extra points.

Drivebase and Power

With an open-field game demanding speed and precision, most teams leaned toward swerve. We stuck with a tank drivetrain and pushed the design to its limit. Through careful tuning, strong driving and a focus on reliability, we became one of the top tank bots in the country.

Shooter

A compact precision shooter that went through extensive testing. We ran countless iterations, adjusting compression, wheel spacing, angles and motor speeds, until we achieved consistent, highly accurate shots into the Speaker.

Intake

A ground intake built for fast cycles, feeding the shooter directly so the robot could pick up and score without repositioning.

Season Highlights

  • Autonomous Award at the Durham College District Event
  • Creativity Award sponsored by Rockwell Automation at North Bay
  • Ontario Provincial Championship, Science Division
